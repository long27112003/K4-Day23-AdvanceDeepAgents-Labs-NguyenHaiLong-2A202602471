# Survey on LLM Agents and Tool Use

## TL;DR
- LLM agents are developed with modular architectures focusing on perception, reasoning, planning, and execution capabilities [1].
- Effective tool utilization in LLM agents requires iterative optimization and structured testing environments [2].
- Evaluation metrics assess safety, optimization capabilities, and effectiveness of prompts, highlighting a need for standardized benchmarks [3].
- Current challenges faced by LLM agents include safety concerns and ethical issues, necessitating robust evaluation frameworks [4].

## Background
Large Language Model (LLM) agents represent a significant advancement in AI, integrating complex architectures that allow for human-like interaction and problem-solving. Such systems are characterized by their abilities to process, reason, and make decisions based on vast amounts of data. The foundational principles guiding the development of LLM architectures focus on clarity in design and functional capabilities, underscored by a need for normative competence to align with human values in AI systems [1].

## Foundational Architectures
LLM agents are built upon a variety of architectural frameworks that allow them to engage in multifaceted tasks. These architectures typically integrate elements of perception, reasoning, and sequential planning, positioned within modular frameworks that facilitate clarity and efficacy in task management [1]. The studies emphasize the ongoing evolution of these frameworks, which are crucial for optimizing performance in real-world applications.

## Tool Utilization
The integration of tools within LLM agents is paramount for enhancing their capabilities. Recent findings indicate that effective tool utilization stems from robust frameworks and thorough testing environments. Iterative optimization processes have been identified as essential for improving the functionality and reliability of LLM agents [2].

## Performance Evaluations
The evaluation of LLM agents incorporates a range of metrics to assess their performance comprehensively. Main areas of assessment include safety protocols, black-box optimization capabilities, and prompt effectiveness. This necessitates the establishment of standardized benchmarks to facilitate comparisons across various studies and applications [3].

## Challenges and Limitations
LLM agents face numerous challenges, particularly in the realm of tool use. Safety concerns regarding the refusal of harmful requests are prominent, and reliability issues may arise due to these agents' inherent limitations in changing environments. Additionally, ethical considerations, such as bias and privacy complications, highlight the pressing need for stringent evaluation frameworks and governance systems [4].

## Trends and Open Problems
Continuous research in the field of LLM agents indicates a growing focus on enhancing their architectural designs and tool use capabilities. Critical issues, such as safety and ethical deployment, remain unsolved challenges that demand urgent attention from the AI community and stakeholders.
